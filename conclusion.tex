\markboth{General Conclusion}{General Conclusion}

The development of the \textbf{Riaya} platform, carried out within the company \textbf{Pengguin} under the leadership of its founder \textbf{John Jang}, represents a significant contribution to the digital transformation of the healthcare sector in Tunisia. This project was born from a concrete observation: despite the growing digitalization of medical services, a large part of the Tunisian population still faces real difficulties in booking medical appointments, whether due to limited internet access, low digital literacy, language barriers, or the urgent nature of certain medical situations. Riaya addresses these challenges by offering an \textbf{AI-powered, voice-first, and multilingual} solution accessible to everyone through a simple phone call and WhatsApp.

\section*{Summary of the Work Completed}

Throughout this report, three main axes were explored.

The \textbf{first chapter} presented the general context of the project. It introduced the host company Pengguin and its founder, described the Riaya product and its value proposition, and provided a detailed analysis of the functional and non-functional requirements for the three main actors: the patient, the doctor, and the administrator. The chapter also compared Riaya with three existing categories of solutions (Doctolib, TunisieDocteur / Mondocteur, and classic IVR systems) and demonstrated that Riaya occupies a unique and relevant position in the Tunisian market by combining voice AI, multilingual support, urgent booking, WhatsApp integration, and geolocation-based best-fit matching. Finally, a global BPMN model was proposed to illustrate the end-to-end business process, covering the standard flow, the urgent flow, and the timeout handling.

The \textbf{second chapter} focused on the project management methodology and the modeling practices adopted by the Pengguin team. The Kanban method was presented in detail, including its principles (visualization, WIP limits, flow management), its advantages, its limitations, and a comparative analysis with Scrum and Waterfall. The justification for choosing Kanban was clearly established: flexibility, continuous delivery, transparency, and the ability to handle unplanned work were essential given the innovative and evolving nature of the project. The chapter then listed and described the main modeling diagrams used in the design of the platform: use case diagram, class/ERD diagram, sequence diagram, activity diagrams, deployment diagram, and the global BPMN model. These diagrams provided a solid foundation for understanding both the functional and technical aspects of Riaya.

The \textbf{third chapter} was dedicated to the technical architecture, implementation, and deployment of the platform. It explained the decision to split the project into two independent repositories — \texttt{riaya-web} (Next.js) and \texttt{riaya-voice} (Express.js) — and detailed the technology stack component by component: Next.js, TailwindCSS, and Better Auth for the web stack; PostgreSQL and Drizzle ORM for the database; Express.js, Twilio, and the OpenAI Realtime API (via Microsoft Azure) for the voice server; WhatsApp Baileys, Cloudflare R2, and Konnect.network for third-party integrations; BullMQ and Redis for background jobs; and Docker, GitHub Actions, Nginx, and a self-hosted Ubuntu VPS for infrastructure and CI/CD. The chapter also presented screenshots of the main features of the platform, discussed security, confidentiality, and access management, and described the technical challenges encountered — particularly real-time latency management, the best-fit algorithm, and the handling of urgencies and timeouts — along with the solutions adopted.

\section*{Challenges Encountered and Lessons Learned}

The Riaya project presented several technical and organizational challenges. On the technical side, the most demanding aspect was undoubtedly the \textbf{real-time AI voice interaction}. Bridging the audio stream between Twilio and the OpenAI Realtime API required a careful design of WebSocket connections, audio buffering strategies, and latency optimization. The use of regional Azure deployments and a dedicated voice server contributed significantly to achieving a smooth and natural conversational experience.

The \textbf{best-fit matching algorithm} was another major challenge. It had to combine geocoding, distance computation using the Haversine formula, and a scoring function that balances proximity and time preference, all within a few hundred milliseconds. The algorithm was successfully optimized to return the top two to three doctors in near real time, ensuring a fluid experience for the patient.

The \textbf{management of urgent requests and timeouts} required the use of delayed jobs (BullMQ), shared emergency group identifiers, and automatic cancellation mechanisms. This design ensured that urgent patients were never blocked by subscription caps, and that unanswered requests were automatically cancelled with a callback or WhatsApp fallback.

On the organizational side, the adoption of \textbf{Kanban} proved particularly well suited to a small, cross-functional team working on an innovative product. It enabled continuous delivery, quick adaptation to feedback, and clear visibility of progress for both the development team and the client.

\section*{Future Perspectives}

Several perspectives for improvement and extension can be considered for the future of Riaya.

First, the platform could be extended to \textbf{other countries in North Africa and the Maghreb}, adapting its language models and local integrations. The modular architecture of the voice server would make such an extension technically feasible.

Second, additional features could further enrich the doctor experience, such as \textbf{teleconsultation via video calls}, \textbf{advanced analytics dashboards} for Pro subscribers, or a \textbf{mobile application} for doctors to manage their practice on the go.

Third, the \textbf{AI assistant for doctors} could be extended with more specialized medical reasoning capabilities, while remaining strictly within the boundaries of medical confidentiality and regulatory compliance.

Finally, the \textbf{AI voice agent} itself could be continuously improved through iterative fine-tuning and the collection of anonymized feedback, allowing it to better understand Tunisian Arabic dialects and handle increasingly complex conversations.

\section*{Personal Contribution}

This project allowed me to acquire and strengthen a wide range of technical and professional skills. On the technical side, I deepened my knowledge of modern web development with Next.js and TailwindCSS, of real-time communication with WebSockets and the OpenAI Realtime API, of cloud-native deployment with Docker and GitHub Actions, and of database design with PostgreSQL and Drizzle ORM. On the methodological side, I learned to apply the Kanban method in a real-world context, to model complex systems using UML and BPMN, and to communicate technical decisions clearly to both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

More generally, this project confirmed my interest in building innovative, AI-driven products that solve concrete problems and have a real impact on people's daily lives. The Riaya platform, by making healthcare more accessible to a broader population, is a perfect illustration of how technology can be used to serve the public good.
