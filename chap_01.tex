\section{Presentation of the Riaya Product (Client of Pengguin)}

\subsection{Definition and Genesis of the Project}
Riaya is an AI-powered doctor appointment platform based in Tunisia. It started from the need to make healthcare easier to reach for patients who are not comfortable with web portals or mobile applications. A Tunisian client initiated the project, and Pengguin developed it.

Instead of asking patients to download an application, create an account, and find their way through a complex interface, Riaya lets them call a phone number and talk to a voice assistant. The assistant understands natural language, collects the information it needs, and books an appointment with the most suitable doctor. Patients then receive confirmation and follow-up messages on WhatsApp, which is already widely used in Tunisia \cite{whatsapp2024tunisia}.

This approach answers several problems in the Tunisian healthcare system. It removes the digital barrier that keeps many patients, especially older adults and people with limited digital literacy, away from online booking. It works in Arabic, French, and English. It handles urgent cases by contacting several nearby doctors at the same time. Doctors, for their part, get a modern dashboard to run their practice.

\subsection{Value Proposition: The ``AI-first'' and ``Voice-first'' Concept}
Patients need no account and no web portal: they talk to a voice assistant over an ordinary phone call and receive follow-up messages on WhatsApp. Because the assistant speaks English, French, and Tunisian Arabic, a wide audience can use the service.

``AI-first'' means the user experience is built around artificial intelligence. The voice agent uses the OpenAI Realtime API \cite{openai2024realtime}, accessed through Microsoft Azure \cite{azure2024openai}, for low-latency speech-to-speech conversation that sounds natural. It understands what the patient wants, asks follow-up questions when something is unclear, and calls tools in the Riaya system to search for doctors, check availability, and create appointments.

``Voice-first'' means the phone call is the main channel. The team chose this on purpose: in Tunisia, phone calls remain a trusted and familiar way to communicate. WhatsApp is the second channel, used after the call for confirmations, documents, invoices, and rating requests.

\subsection{The Three Pillars of the Platform: Patient, Doctor, Administrator}
The platform is built around three main actors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient:} No account required. Interacts via phone calls and WhatsApp. The phone number is used as the unique identifier. The patient can book standard or urgent appointments, consult or cancel pending bookings, receive WhatsApp messages from the doctor, and rate the doctor after a visit.
    \item \textbf{Doctor:} Uses a web dashboard to manage appointments, patients, invoices, and WhatsApp communication. The doctor must register, submit professional documents, and be approved by an administrator before accessing the full dashboard. Once approved, the doctor can set availability, accept or decline AI bookings, create invoices, record and transcribe consultations, use the AI assistant, and link a personal WhatsApp number.
    \item \textbf{Administrator:} Uses a back-office to verify doctors, monitor live AI calls, and configure the platform. The administrator can approve or reject doctor applications, observe ongoing calls with live transcripts, run best-fit searches, and consult global statistics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Business Model (Freemium: Free vs Pro)}
Riaya offers doctors two subscription plans: Free and Pro. The Free plan gives access to the main dashboard features, including patient and appointment management, invoices, and medical files. Advanced features are capped each month: five AI phone bookings, fifty WhatsApp messages, five conversation recordings, and twenty AI messages.

The Pro plan removes these limits. Doctors can upgrade at any time and then pay recurring invoices to keep Pro active. Payments go through the Konnect.network API \cite{konnect2024}. A daily BullMQ job \cite{bullmq2024} finds subscriptions that are about to end and generates their invoices, which doctors see and pay from their dashboard.

With this freemium model, doctors can try the platform without commitment and upgrade when they need more capacity, and the paid plan gives the platform the revenue it needs to keep running and improving.

\section{Functional Requirements}

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Patient (No Account)}
Patients have no account; the calling phone number identifies them. They use two channels, the phone call and WhatsApp. Their functional requirements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Voice Interaction with AI:} Reach the AI in English, French, or Tunisian Arabic. Change preferred language for current and future interactions. The assistant does not give medical advice.
    \item \textbf{Appointment Booking (Standard and Urgent):} Provide name (if unknown), reason or speciality, location, and preferred time. Choose among best-fit doctors within a 50~km radius. A pending appointment is created. Urgent requests can contact up to 3 nearby doctors simultaneously.
    \item \textbf{Consultation and Cancellation of Pending Appointments:} Ask about upcoming or recent AI appointments. Cancel while status is pending. Confirmed bookings cannot be cancelled via the assistant.
    \item \textbf{Receiving WhatsApp Messages:} Receive appointment confirmations, medical documents, and invoices from the doctor's own WhatsApp number.
    \item \textbf{Rating System via Magic Link:} Open a magic link received by WhatsApp after a confirmed appointment and submit a rating without creating an account.
    \item \textbf{Notification on Request Expiry:} Be notified by callback or WhatsApp if a pending request expires without a doctor accepting.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Doctor}
The doctor uses a web dashboard. The requirements fall into these categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Registration and Verification:} Create an account with email OTP. Submit professional and cabinet details (identity documents, Medical Council info, speciality, location on map, etc.). The account must be approved by an administrator. If rejected, the doctor can correct the information and reapply.
    \item \textbf{Profile, Availability, and Calendar Management:} Update cabinet and professional information. Set weekly working hours used when patients book via AI. Create, move, edit, or cancel appointments on the practice calendar.
    \item \textbf{Patient and Medical Record Management:} Create and update patient records. Attach patients created from AI bookings. Create consultation notes, prescriptions, and other medical files.
    \item \textbf{Invoicing and Sending via WhatsApp:} Create invoices, record payments, and cancel invoices as needed. Send invoice PDF to the patient via WhatsApp.
    \item \textbf{Recording and Transcription of Consultations:} Record a conversation with a patient. Generate a transcript from a recording.
    \item \textbf{Integrated AI Assistant:} Ask how to use Riaya and what is allowed under the current plan. Ask medical questions. Attach a recording transcript and ask questions about it.
    \item \textbf{WhatsApp Configuration (QR Code):} Link a personal WhatsApp number by scanning a QR code so outbound messages to patients use that number \cite{baileys2024}.
    \item \textbf{Subscription Management:} View Free vs Pro limits and current usage. Upgrade and pay recurring Riaya invoices (Konnect) to keep Pro active.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Administrator}
The administrator manages the platform from a dedicated back-office. The requirements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Doctor Verification and Validation:} Browse and search doctor applications and profiles. Approve or reject a doctor application (rejection may include reasons shown to the doctor).
    \item \textbf{Real-Time Monitoring of AI Calls:} Observe live patient AI calls and conversation transcripts. Inspect best-fit results for operations and debugging.
    \item \textbf{``Best-fit'' Search and Platform Management:} Manage platform-level data exposed in the admin interface (e.g., users, specialities, settings). Link the admin WhatsApp number for platform outbound messages.
    \item \textbf{Consultation of Global Statistics:} Consult aggregate statistics on doctors, appointments, and calls.
\end{itemize}

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}

\subsection{Performance and Real-Time}
The platform uses WebSockets \cite{fette2011websocket} to notify doctors of new appointment requests in real time. A pending AI request is cancelled automatically if no doctor accepts it within 10 minutes. The best-fit search algorithm is optimized to run within a few hundred milliseconds, so the patient is not kept waiting. The voice server must relay audio between Twilio \cite{twilio2024} and the OpenAI Realtime API with minimal delay to keep the conversation natural.

\subsection{Security and Confidentiality}
Doctors authenticate with Better Auth \cite{betterauth2024} using an email OTP, stored in the Drizzle verification table \cite{drizzle2024}. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest. Medical documents are stored in Cloudflare R2 \cite{cloudflare2024r2}, which provides encryption and access control. WhatsApp conversations stay private because they go through the doctor's own number. The platform must comply with Tunisian data protection regulations (INPDP) \cite{inpdp2004} and, where applicable, GDPR principles \cite{gdpr2016}.

\subsection{Scalability and High Availability}
The architecture is split into two repositories: the web app (Next.js \cite{nextjs2024}) and the real-time voice server (Express.js \cite{express2024}). Each can be scaled and maintained on its own, and each has its own CI/CD pipeline, so the team can deploy one application without redeploying the other. Docker containers and Docker Compose \cite{docker2024} simplify scaling and infrastructure management.

\subsection{Resilience and Fault Tolerance}
If an outbound call fails, Riaya sends a WhatsApp message from the admin WhatsApp account instead. BullMQ and Redis \cite{redis2024} run the background jobs, so scheduled tasks such as review requests and recurring invoices are processed reliably. The emergency fan-out mechanism makes sure subscription caps never block an urgent patient, and the timeout mechanism cancels unanswered requests and notifies the patient.

\subsection{Ergonomics and User Experience (UX/UI)}
The doctor dashboard uses TailwindCSS \cite{tailwind2024} for a modern, responsive interface. Patients install nothing and create no account. The AI assistant speaks naturally and asks one question at a time, and WhatsApp messages go out in the patient's preferred language.

\subsection{Technical and Regulatory Constraints}
Location search is restricted to Tunisia, and the platform runs on Tunisian local time (GMT+1). Each doctor's cabinet is stored with the exact latitude and longitude picked on the registration map, so matching compares GPS coordinates instead of city names. The platform must comply with local data protection regulations (INPDP) and, where applicable, with the GDPR principles of data minimization, purpose limitation, and the right to erasure.

\section{Existing Solutions}
Before designing Riaya, the Pengguin team studied the existing medical appointment booking solutions. They fall into three categories: international web and mobile platforms, local Tunisian directories, and classic Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems \cite{ivr2019}. For each one, the team looked at its strengths, its limitations, and the gaps Riaya could fill. This section presents each solution, its features, and why it does not fully meet the needs of the Tunisian market.

\subsection{Solution 1: Doctolib (Classic Web/Mobile Platform)}
Doctolib \cite{doctolib2024} is one of the leading European platforms for online medical appointment booking. It was founded in France in 2013 and now dominates the French, German, and Italian markets, with millions of users and tens of thousands of registered healthcare professionals.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
Doctolib's features serve patients, doctors, and the wider healthcare system:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{For patients:} Doctor search by speciality, location, and availability; online appointment booking; appointment reminders by email and SMS; access to medical documents; and teleconsultation services.
    \item \textbf{For doctors:} A complete practice management dashboard, including calendar management, patient records, billing, and integration with third-party software.
    \item \textbf{For the healthcare system:} Integration with national health insurance systems and electronic health records in some countries.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Strengths}
Doctolib's main strengths are its easy-to-use interface, its large network of healthcare professionals, and its integration with the wider healthcare system. It has become the de facto standard for online booking in France and has expanded successfully to other European countries.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why It Does Not Fit the Tunisian Context}
Doctolib works well in Europe, but several limitations make it unsuitable for the Tunisian market:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Requirement for a patient account:} Patients must create an account and use a web or mobile interface. This excludes a significant portion of the Tunisian population, particularly older adults, individuals with limited digital literacy, and those without reliable internet access.
    \item \textbf{No voice-based AI assistant:} Doctolib does not offer a voice assistant for non-connected users. All interactions must go through the web or mobile application.
    \item \textbf{No WhatsApp integration:} WhatsApp is the most widely used messaging platform in Tunisia, but Doctolib does not integrate with it.
    \item \textbf{No urgent booking flow:} Doctolib does not provide a mechanism for urgent or emergency bookings that contact multiple doctors simultaneously.
    \item \textbf{Not adapted to the Tunisian market:} Doctolib is designed for European healthcare systems and does not support Tunisian-specific features such as local payment methods (Konnect.network) or Tunisian Arabic language support.
    \item \textbf{Language limitations:} While Doctolib supports multiple languages, it does not support Tunisian Arabic, which is the preferred language for many patients.
\end{itemize}

Doctolib serves European markets well, but it does not address the needs and constraints of Tunisian healthcare.

\subsection{Solution 2: TunisieDocteur / Mondocteur (Local Directories)}
TunisieDocteur \cite{tunisiedoctor2024} and Mondocteur are two of the best-known Tunisian platforms for finding a doctor and booking online. They work as directories that list doctors by speciality, location, and other criteria, and patients can request appointments through them.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
These platforms offer:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Doctor directory:} A searchable database of doctors with information about their speciality, location, and contact details.
    \item \textbf{Appointment request:} Patients can send an appointment request to a doctor, who then confirms or declines it.
    \item \textbf{Patient reviews:} Some platforms include reviews and ratings from other patients.
    \item \textbf{Basic search filters:} Search by speciality, city, and doctor name.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Strengths}
Their main strengths are their local focus, their knowledge of the Tunisian market, and their connection to local doctors. Patients who are comfortable with web interfaces find them fairly easy to use.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why They Do Not Fully Address the Need}
Their limitations are significant:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Requirement for internet access and account creation:} Patients must have internet access and create an account, which excludes a portion of the population.
    \item \textbf{No voice interaction:} There is no voice-based AI assistant. All interactions are text-based and require the patient to navigate a web interface.
    \item \textbf{No urgent booking:} There is no mechanism for urgent or emergency bookings that contact multiple doctors simultaneously.
    \item \textbf{No WhatsApp integration:} There is no integration with WhatsApp, which is the preferred messaging platform for many Tunisians.
    \item \textbf{Limited real-time features:} The platforms do not offer real-time notifications to doctors or live call monitoring for administrators.
    \item \textbf{No intelligent best-fit matching:} The search is based on simple filters and does not use geolocation or a scoring algorithm to find the best doctor for the patient.
    \item \textbf{Limited multilingual support:} While some platforms support French and Arabic, they do not offer full multilingual voice interaction.
\end{itemize}

These platforms are closer to what the Tunisian market needs, but they lack Riaya's accessibility, real-time interaction, and intelligent matching.

\subsection{Solution 3: Classic IVR Systems (Interactive Voice Response)}
Some hospitals and clinics in Tunisia use classic Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems to handle appointment requests. These systems are automated phone menus that allow callers to select options using their phone keypad \cite{ivr2019}.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
Classic IVR systems usually offer:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Automated menu:} Callers are presented with a menu of options (e.g., ``Press 1 for appointments, Press 2 for information'').
    \item \textbf{Keypad input:} Callers navigate the menu by pressing numbers on their phone keypad.
    \item \textbf{Basic routing:} Calls can be routed to a specific department or to a human operator.
    \item \textbf{Appointment scheduling:} Some systems allow callers to schedule appointments by entering a date and time using the keypad.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Strengths}
Classic IVR systems are simple and cheap, and they handle large volumes of calls without human intervention. Anyone with a phone can use them, regardless of internet access or digital literacy.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why They Are Insufficient}
Their accessibility comes with significant limitations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Rigid menu structure:} Callers must navigate a fixed menu of options, which can be frustrating and time-consuming.
    \item \textbf{No natural language understanding:} The system cannot understand natural speech. It only responds to keypad input.
    \item \textbf{No geolocation:} The system cannot determine the caller's location or find nearby doctors.
    \item \textbf{No urgent booking:} There is no mechanism for urgent or emergency bookings.
    \item \textbf{No WhatsApp integration:} There is no integration with WhatsApp for confirmations or follow-up messages.
    \item \textbf{No intelligent matching:} The system cannot match patients with the best-fit doctor based on distance, availability, or speciality.
    \item \textbf{No multilingual support:} Most IVR systems only support one language, typically French or Arabic, but not both, and certainly not English.
    \item \textbf{Poor user experience:} Long menus and repetitive prompts can lead to caller frustration and abandoned calls.
\end{itemize}

Classic IVR systems reach a wide audience, but they lack the intelligence, flexibility, and real-time capabilities that patients and doctors now expect.

\section{BPMN Modeling of the Complete Project}

\subsection{Description of Business Flows (Standard, Urgent, Timeout)}
The BPMN model \cite{omg2011bpmn} covers three main flows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Standard Flow:} The patient calls, the AI gathers information (name, reason, location, preferred time), a best-fit search is run, the patient selects a doctor, a pending appointment is created, and the doctor accepts or declines. If accepted, a WhatsApp confirmation is sent to the patient. If declined, the appointment is cancelled.
    \item \textbf{Urgent Flow:} The AI detects urgency, confirms with the patient, creates up to 3 urgent pending appointments (emergency group), the first doctor to accept wins, and the others are cancelled automatically. If the patient later cancels one of the urgent requests, the whole group is cancelled.
    \item \textbf{Timeout Flow:} If no doctor accepts within 10 minutes, the pending request is auto-cancelled. The platform calls the patient back or sends a WhatsApp message asking them to call again to book with another doctor.
\end{itemize}
