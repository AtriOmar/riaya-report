\section{Presentation of the Riaya Product (Client of Pengguin)}

\subsection{Definition and Genesis of the Project}
Riaya is an AI doctor appointment platform made for Tunisia. The project started from the observation that many patients are not comfortable with web portals or mobile applications, while almost everyone can make a phone call.

Patients book an appointment by calling a phone line and talking, without installing an application or creating an account. Doctors use a web tool to run their practice: their agenda, their patients and medical files, and their invoices. The phone line can only book doctors who keep their agenda up to date in that tool, so each side depends on the other.

\subsection{Value Proposition: The ``AI-first'' and ``Voice-first'' Concept}
``Voice-first'' means the phone call is the main channel for patients. We chose this on purpose: in Tunisia, a phone call is a familiar and trusted way to reach a cabinet. WhatsApp, already widely used in Tunisia \cite{whatsapp2024tunisia}, is the second channel, used after the call for confirmations, medical documents, invoices and rating links. Patients never need a website.

``AI-first'' means the patient side is built around an AI agent instead of menus or forms. The voice agent uses the Gemini Live API \cite{gemini2026live} with the Gemini 3.1 Flash Live preview model. It listens and answers with its own voice, so the conversation flows like a normal call and the patient can interrupt it. We picked it after comparing several realtime models and chains of separate speech-to-text, language and text-to-speech models; Chapter~3 explains that choice. During the call the agent uses tools in the Riaya system to look up places, search for doctors and free slots, book or cancel requests and save the caller's name and language. It keeps its replies short, asks one question at a time and never gives medical advice.

The doctor side uses AI as well. A doctor can record a consultation in the browser, get a transcript that separates the speakers, and ask an AI assistant about medical questions, the product or the practice's own data. The assistant can prepare an invoice or a booking decision, but nothing changes until the doctor confirms it.

\subsection{The Actors of the Platform}
The platform has five actors: the patient, the visitor, the doctor, the secretary who works on behalf of a doctor, and the administrator.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Patient:} has no account and uses only the phone and WhatsApp. The calling number identifies the line. The patient can book standard or urgent appointments for themselves or for a family member on the same phone, list or cancel pending bookings, and rate the doctor after a visit. The AI assistant handles the rest of the call, such as the language, the callbacks and the WhatsApp messages.
    \item \textbf{Visitor:} someone who has not signed up yet, usually a doctor discovering the platform. The visitor can only sign up, with an email code, and then becomes a doctor who applies for verification.
    \item \textbf{Doctor:} signs up on the website, submits professional documents and waits for an administrator's approval. Once verified, the doctor sets weekly availability, accepts or refuses phone bookings, manages patients, medical files and invoices, records consultations, uses the AI assistant, links their own WhatsApp number and manages the subscription.
    \item \textbf{Secretary:} is invited by email by a doctor on the Pro plan. A secretary handles appointments, patients, invoices and availability for that doctor, without access to medical files, recordings, the AI assistant or billing.
    \item \textbf{Administrator:} runs the platform from a back-office. The administrator verifies doctors, monitors live calls, reviews calls reported as abusive, blocks numbers, runs best-fit searches, manages specialities and users, links the platform WhatsApp account and reads platform statistics.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Business Model (Freemium: Free vs Pro)}
Riaya offers doctors two plans, Free and Pro. Both give the main practice features: patients, appointments, availability, invoices and medical files. Phone booking by the AI assistant is unlimited on both plans, so a patient is never turned away because of the plan a doctor chose. The plans differ on the features that cost Riaya money to run: WhatsApp messages started by the doctor, recording and transcription time, and the AI assistant.

On the Free plan a doctor can send 30 WhatsApp messages a month, record and transcribe 2 hours of consultations, and ask the AI assistant about 20 questions. The Pro plan costs 49~TND per month. It removes the WhatsApp limit, gives 100 hours of recording, and allows about 2,000 assistant questions. Pro doctors can also buy extra recording hours in packs (25 hours for 15~TND, 100 hours for 50~TND), get an AI consultation note on each transcribed recording, add secretaries and print their logo and stamp on invoices.

Doctors can upgrade at any time. Payments go through the Konnect.network API \cite{konnect2024}. Konnect has no built-in subscriptions, so Riaya manages the cycle itself: a daily BullMQ job \cite{bullmq2024} at 08:00 UTC finds Pro subscriptions that end within three days and creates the next invoice with a payment link. The doctor pays it from the Subscription page to keep Pro. A Pro plan that is not renewed falls back to Free.

\subsection{How the Limits Are Counted}\label{subsec:usage-limits}

The Free and Pro plans limit three things that cost us money: WhatsApp sends, recording time and use of the AI assistant. Each one needed its own counting rule. Phone booking by the AI has no limit on any plan, because limiting it would punish patients for a doctor's plan. \xref{fig:plan_limits} shows how an action is checked.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=0.72\textheight,keepaspectratio]{images/diagrams/plan_limits_flow.png}
    \caption{How a doctor's action is checked against the Free and Pro limits}
    \label{fig:plan_limits}
\end{figure}

The first step is the effective plan. A doctor is on Pro only if the subscription is Pro, its status is active, and its period end is still ahead; a missing, unpaid or expired subscription behaves as Free. For WhatsApp, only the sends the doctor starts count, with a limit of 30 a month on Free and none on Pro. A medical file with documents counts as one caption plus one per document, a text-only file counts as one, and an invoice PDF counts as one. Automatic messages to patients, such as confirmations, review requests and timeout or refusal messages, are never counted and never blocked. The check and the increment happen in one SQL statement, so two sends at the same moment cannot both pass the limit.

Recording time is counted when a recording is transcribed, and re-running a transcription does not count again. Free includes 2 hours a month and Pro 100 hours, and Pro doctors can buy packs of 25 hours for 15~TND or 100 hours for 50~TND. The monthly hours are used first, then the pack hours, which never expire. A database lock per doctor stops two charges from racing, and usage rows stay after a recording is deleted, so deleting never gives time back. A recording is always saved, even with no time left. It is then marked as not transcribed, and the doctor can transcribe it later with one click.

The AI assistant is limited by its real cost and not by the number of messages, because a long chat with a transcript attached costs far more than a short question. OpenRouter reports the cost of each reply, and the app compares the month's total with a budget of \$0.05 on Free and \$5 on Pro. A typical question costs about \$0.0025, which gives the ``about 20'' and ``about 2,000'' questions shown in the offer. Doctors only see their usage as a percentage. Since the cost of a reply is known only after it ends, the last reply of the month can go slightly over the budget. All counters reset at the start of each UTC calendar month.

\subsection{Free Doctors in the Best-Fit Search}
\label{subsec:free-doctors-bestfit}

When we designed the plans, we considered leaving Free doctors out of the best-fit results, the ranked list of nearby doctors that the AI offers on the phone (Section~\ref{subsec:bestfit}), so that doctors would have one more reason to subscribe to Pro. We dropped the idea. A patient who calls Riaya should always be offered the doctor who best matches their need, place and time, whatever plan that doctor pays for. Hiding a closer doctor to sell subscriptions would make the service worse for the people it is built for. Free and Pro doctors are ranked the same way, and the plans only limit features on the doctor's side.

\subsection{Pricing Display Decision}
\label{subsec:pricing-display}

Price matters when choosing a doctor, and at first we wanted to show doctors' prices on the platform. If only the price of a first consultation mattered, this would be easy. The real cost, however, depends on many things, and two doctors with the same consultation price can charge very different amounts for what follows. We considered letting doctors enter a price for each service, which works for services with a fixed price, such as braces. In most cases, though, patients do not know what they need before a diagnosis, and an AI cannot make that diagnosis over the phone. We therefore decided not to show full prices. A partial price could mislead patients, and the platform's job is to book the visit.

\section{Functional Requirements}

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Patient (No Account)}
Patients have no account; the calling phone number identifies the line, and the assistant never asks for it. They use two channels, the phone call and WhatsApp. Their requirements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Voice interaction with the AI:} talk to the assistant in Tunisian Derja, French or English. Derja is the default; the patient can switch language, and the choice is kept for later calls and WhatsApp messages. Mixing a few French words into Derja does not count as a switch. The assistant gives no medical advice or diagnosis.
    \item \textbf{Standard booking:} give a name if the assistant does not know it, the reason or the speciality, the place and the preferred time. The assistant confirms the speciality, proposes two or three doctors within 50~km with the distance in kilometres and the time, and keeps searching until the patient chooses or stops. The booking is a pending request, so the assistant says it was sent, not confirmed.
    \item \textbf{Booking a specific doctor:} name a doctor directly and get that doctor's free slots, without giving a speciality or a city.
    \item \textbf{Urgent booking:} for urgent symptoms, accept to send requests to up to three of the closest available doctors at once. For life-threatening signs such as chest pain, trouble breathing or heavy bleeding, the assistant first tells the patient to call the SAMU at 190, then offers the urgent requests.
    \item \textbf{Listing and cancelling bookings:} ask about upcoming or recent phone bookings and cancel one while it is still pending. A confirmed appointment can only be changed through the doctor's office.
    \item \textbf{WhatsApp messages:} receive confirmations, medical documents and invoices from the doctor's own WhatsApp number, in the patient's preferred language.
    \item \textbf{Callbacks:} be called back if a doctor refuses the request or does not answer within ten minutes. After a refusal the callback offers up to three other doctors. If the patient does not pick up, the same message arrives on WhatsApp.
    \item \textbf{Rating:} two hours after the visit, open a link received on WhatsApp and rate the doctor (rating, waiting time, comment) without an account.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Booking for Family Members}
One phone often serves a whole family, so the assistant asks who the appointment is for when other people are known on the line. A known family member is booked directly. A new one is added with a first name, a last name and the relationship to the phone owner (child, parent, spouse, sibling or other). The reason for the visit always describes the patient, not the caller. If the caller is not the phone owner, for example a wife using her husband's phone, the assistant adds her as a family member and leaves the owner's profile unchanged.

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Doctor}
The doctor uses a web dashboard. The requirements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Registration and verification:} create an account with an email one-time code, then submit an application with identity card photos, speciality, Medical Council number and certificate, tax ID, cabinet details and the cabinet position chosen on a map. Until an administrator approves it, only the Profile page is open. A rejected doctor sees the reasons, fixes the data and applies again.
    \item \textbf{Availability and calendar:} set weekly working hours, which the AI uses when it searches for slots. Create, move, edit or cancel appointments on a 30-minute calendar grid. Accept or refuse phone bookings, which appear live with a sound, and with a red badge when they are urgent.
    \item \textbf{Patients and medical files:} create and update patient records, link a phone booking to a patient file when accepting it, and store consultation notes, prescriptions and lab reports with attached documents.
    \item \textbf{Invoicing:} create invoices, record cash or transfer payments, cancel invoices, and send the PDF to the patient on WhatsApp.
    \item \textbf{Consultation recordings:} record a consultation in the browser, get a transcript with the speakers separated, and on Pro an AI consultation note in French.
    \item \textbf{AI assistant:} ask medical or product questions, attach a transcript, and ask about the schedule, patients or invoices. The assistant can propose an invoice, a booking decision, a note, an appointment or a move, and the change only happens after the doctor confirms it.
    \item \textbf{WhatsApp link:} link the doctor's own WhatsApp number by scanning a QR code, so messages to patients leave from that number \cite{baileys2024}.
    \item \textbf{Subscription:} see the plan limits and current usage, upgrade, pay the monthly Riaya invoice, and buy extra recording hours.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Secretary}
A doctor on the Pro plan can invite any number of secretaries by email from the Team page. The invitation link is valid for seven days and can be sent again or cancelled. The secretary signs up or signs in with the invited email address and joins the practice. A secretary can then use the dashboard home, patients (without medical files), appointments, invoices and availability for that doctor. Invoices a secretary sends still leave from the doctor's WhatsApp number. Medical files, recordings, the AI assistant, WhatsApp setup, the subscription and the team page stay reserved to the doctor. If the doctor goes back to the Free plan, the secretary's access is paused, and it comes back when Pro is renewed.

\subsection{Functional Requirements of the Administrator}
The administrator manages the platform from a dedicated back-office. The requirements are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Doctor verification:} browse and search applications, open the documents, then approve, reject with reasons, or ban.
    \item \textbf{Live call monitoring:} follow ongoing calls with the live transcript, the tool calls and the booking status, and listen to recordings of past calls.
    \item \textbf{Best-fit search:} run the same doctor search as the phone assistant on a map and a calendar, and open any doctor's availability.
    \item \textbf{Platform data:} manage users and the list of specialities, and link the platform WhatsApp account used for system messages.
    \item \textbf{Abuse handling:} review the calls the AI reported, with the recording and the transcript, then dismiss the report, reopen it, or block the number for 7 days, 30 days or permanently. A block can be shortened, extended or lifted later.
    \item \textbf{Statistics:} read platform statistics on doctors, appointments and calls.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Abuse Handling and Blocked Numbers}
A public phone number attracts some prank and abusive calls. The assistant gives one calm warning for mocking or insults and reports the call at once for threats or sexual talk. It never reports confusion, frustration, elderly callers, children, bad lines or questions such as ``are you a robot''; when in doubt it does not report. After a report it says a neutral goodbye and hangs up, without telling the caller about the report. Each report holds a category, a short summary written by the AI and a review status, and it appears in the administrator's queue with a counter in the sidebar. Only an administrator can block a number. A blocked caller is rejected with a busy signal before any AI session starts, and receives no callback and no WhatsApp message.

\section{Non-Functional Requirements}

\subsection{Performance and Real-Time}
A phone conversation only feels natural if the assistant answers quickly, so the voice server relays audio between Twilio \cite{twilio2024} and the Gemini Live API as a continuous stream, with no intermediate text step. The best-fit search runs while the patient waits on the line. It filters doctors by straight-line distance first and only asks a routing service for road distances for the eight best candidates, in one call with a 3-second limit. New requests reach the doctor's calendar through WebSockets \cite{fette2011websocket}, so the doctor sees them without reloading the page. Work that does not need to happen during the call, such as timeouts, callbacks and review requests, runs in background jobs.

\subsection{Security and Confidentiality}
Doctors sign up with an email one-time code (six digits, valid ten minutes, five attempts) and sign in with email and password through Better Auth \cite{betterauth2024}. The account is created only after the code is verified, and until then the password is kept encrypted. Every API route checks the role of the caller, and secretaries are kept away from medical data. Calls from the voice server to the web app carry a shared secret, and Twilio's recording webhook is checked against Twilio's signature. Documents and recordings are stored in Cloudflare R2 \cite{cloudflare2024r2} and uploaded with short-lived signed URLs. Requests to the doctor AI assistant may contain patient data, so they are only routed to providers that neither keep nor train on them. WhatsApp messages to patients leave from the doctor's own number, which keeps the conversation between the doctor and the patient. The platform must also respect the Tunisian data protection law (INPDP) \cite{inpdp2004} and, where applicable, the principles of the GDPR \cite{gdpr2016}.

\subsection{Scalability and High Availability}
The code is split into two repositories: the web application (Next.js \cite{nextjs2024}) and the real-time voice server (Express.js \cite{express2024}). Each one has its own CI/CD pipeline and its own Docker image, so we can deploy one without touching the other. Both run in Docker containers managed with Docker Compose \cite{docker2024}, next to PostgreSQL and Redis containers. Background work goes through BullMQ queues on Redis \cite{redis2024}, which keeps the web requests short.

\subsection{Resilience and Fault Tolerance}
External services can fail, and a failure should never leave a patient without an answer. If the callback call cannot be placed or the patient does not pick up, Riaya sends the same message on WhatsApp from the platform account. If the road-distance service is slow or fails, the search keeps the straight-line order. The blocked-number check at the start of a call fails open: if the web app does not answer within about 1.5 seconds, the call goes through. Unanswered requests are cancelled after ten minutes by a delayed job, so a patient never waits on a request that nobody will handle. A call watchdog handles silent callers: after 12 seconds of silence the assistant asks once whether the caller is still there, and after 10 more seconds it says goodbye and hangs up. Every call ends politely after 15 minutes at most. Finally, no plan limit ever blocks a patient's booking.

\subsection{Ergonomics and User Experience (UX/UI)}
Patients install nothing and create no account. The assistant uses one or two sentences per turn, asks one question at a time, speaks only Tunisian Derja among the Arabic dialects, and says distances in kilometres. WhatsApp messages go out in the patient's preferred language. The doctor dashboard is built with Tailwind CSS \cite{tailwind2024}, works on mobile and desktop, shows errors with clear messages and keeps drafts in forms. Consultation recordings are also kept in the browser while they are made, so a closed tab does not lose the audio.

\subsection{Technical and Regulatory Constraints}
Patients speak in Tunisia time (GMT+1), while the system stores and exchanges every time in UTC; the assistant converts in both directions, and the doctor assistant works in Africa/Tunis wall-clock time. Plan counters reset on the UTC calendar month. Place search is restricted to Tunisia. Each cabinet is stored with the exact latitude and longitude picked on the registration map, so the search compares coordinates instead of city names. The assistant must speak Derja and never another Arabic dialect, and it pronounces doctor names in Arabic. Amounts are stored in millimes (1~TND = 1,000 millimes) to avoid rounding errors. The platform must comply with the INPDP rules and, where applicable, with the GDPR principles of data minimization, purpose limitation and the right to erasure.

\section{Existing Solutions}
Before designing Riaya, we looked at the existing ways to book a medical appointment. They fall into three groups: international web and mobile platforms, local Tunisian directories, and classic Interactive Voice Response (IVR) systems \cite{voicexml2004}. For each one we noted its features, its strengths and what it leaves out for Tunisian patients.

\subsection{Solution 1: Doctolib (Classic Web/Mobile Platform)}
Doctolib \cite{doctolib2024} is one of the main European platforms for online medical appointments. It was founded in France in 2013 and is widely used in France, Germany and Italy.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
For patients, Doctolib offers doctor search by speciality, location and availability, online booking, reminders by email and SMS, access to medical documents and teleconsultation. For doctors, it provides a practice management dashboard with a calendar, patient records, billing and connections to other software. In some countries it also connects to national health insurance and electronic health record systems.

\subsubsection{Strengths}
Doctolib is easy to use, has a large network of healthcare professionals and is integrated with the health systems of the countries where it operates. In France it has become the usual way to book a doctor online.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why It Does Not Fit the Tunisian Context}
Doctolib is designed for European healthcare systems. Patients must create an account and use the website or the mobile application, which leaves out people who are not comfortable with apps or who have unreliable internet access. There is no voice assistant for patients who cannot or do not want to use the app, and no Tunisian Arabic. It does not use WhatsApp for follow-up messages, and it has no flow that sends an urgent request to several doctors at once.

\subsection{Solution 2: TunisieDocteur / Mondocteur (Local Directories)}
TunisieDocteur \cite{tunisiedoctor2024} and Mondocteur are two well-known Tunisian websites for finding a doctor. They work as directories that list doctors by speciality and city, and patients can request an appointment through them.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
These platforms offer a searchable list of doctors with their speciality, address and contact details, simple filters by speciality, city and name, and appointment requests that the doctor confirms or declines. Some of them also show patient reviews.

\subsubsection{Strengths}
They know the Tunisian market and list local doctors. Patients who are comfortable with websites find them fairly easy to use.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why They Do Not Fully Address the Need}
The patient needs internet access and, to book, an account. All interaction is text on a web page, with no voice assistant. Search relies on simple filters instead of ranking doctors by distance and the nearest free slot, and there is no way to send an urgent request to several doctors at once. Follow-up does not go through WhatsApp, and while some of these sites are available in French and Arabic, none offers a spoken conversation in Derja.

\subsection{Solution 3: Classic IVR Systems (Interactive Voice Response)}
Some hospitals and clinics handle appointment calls with a classic IVR system, an automated phone menu where the caller picks options with the keypad or short spoken commands \cite{voicexml2004}.

\subsubsection{Features and Functionality}
A classic IVR plays a menu (``press 1 for appointments, press 2 for information''), reads the keys the caller presses, and routes the call to a department or a human operator. Some systems also let the caller choose a date and time with the keypad.

\subsubsection{Strengths}
IVR systems are simple and cheap, and they handle many calls without staff. Anyone with a phone can use them, whatever their internet access or digital skills.

\subsubsection{Limitations and Why They Are Insufficient}
The caller has to go through a fixed menu, which is slow and often frustrating, and long menus lead people to hang up. The system does not understand speech, so it cannot take a description of symptoms. It does not know where the caller is, cannot find the nearest available doctor and has no urgent flow. It sends no WhatsApp follow-up, and most systems offer one or two languages at best.

\subsection{Conclusion of the Analysis}
The comparison shows a gap in the Tunisian market. Doctolib is built for European health systems and requires an account. Local directories such as TunisieDocteur and Mondocteur know the market but still need a website, an account and internet access. Classic IVR systems reach everyone with a phone but cannot understand speech or find a doctor. Riaya sits between these: it keeps the phone call that everyone already uses, and adds a voice assistant that understands Derja, ranks doctors by distance and free slot, and follows up on WhatsApp.

\section{BPMN Modeling of the Complete Project}

\subsection{Description of Business Flows (Standard, Urgent, Refusal and Timeout)}
We modeled the booking process in BPMN 2.0 \cite{omg2011bpmn}. The model has two parts, stacked in \xref{fig:bpmn_global}. Part A goes from the call to the pending request and uses four lanes: Patient, AI system, Doctor and Administrator. Part B covers what happens after the request. In part B, the patient and the doctor are drawn as closed pools that only exchange messages with Riaya, and the Riaya pool has two lanes, AI system and Server and jobs. A link event joins the end of part A to the start of part B.

\paragraph{Standard flow.} The process starts when the patient calls. A first gateway checks whether the number is blocked; if it is, the call ends at once, rejected as busy. Otherwise the AI greets the caller and asks for the reason, the place and the time. A gateway then checks for urgent signs. Without them, the AI runs the best-fit search, offers two or three doctors and creates one pending request. The doctor sees the request live on the calendar, and the flow continues in part B. There, an event-based gateway waits for whichever comes first of three events. When the message ``doctor accepts'' arrives, Riaya confirms the appointment, links it to the patient file and sends a WhatsApp confirmation. A timer then fires two hours after the visit and Riaya sends the review link, which ends the process.

\paragraph{Urgent flow.} When the urgent-signs gateway answers yes, the AI advises the patient to call the SAMU at 190 and offers urgent requests before running the same best-fit search. A second gateway then sends the urgent case to a task that creates up to three urgent requests in one group. Both branches merge before the doctor's task, where urgent requests carry a badge. The diagram keeps the urgent case at this level of detail. In the application, the first doctor who accepts keeps the booking and the other requests of the group are cancelled automatically; a refusal from one doctor only cancels that doctor's request, and a patient who cancels one urgent request cancels the whole group.

\paragraph{Refusal and timeout flow.} In part B, the two other events of the gateway lead to a cancellation. If ten minutes pass with no answer, a timer event cancels the request. If the message ``doctor refuses'' arrives, Riaya cancels the request and keeps what the patient asked for in Redis for 24 hours. Both paths merge, and a gateway checks the number again: a blocked number gets nothing. Otherwise the AI calls the patient back, offering up to three other doctors after a refusal, or asking the patient to call again after a timeout. If the patient does not answer, Riaya sends the same message on WhatsApp from the platform account. For an urgent request, a refusal sends no callback, since other doctors in the group can still accept; the figure leaves this case out to stay readable.

\paragraph{Abuse handling.} The task where the AI collects the reason, place and time carries an escalation boundary event named ``Abuse''. When the caller becomes abusive, the AI reports the call and says goodbye. The report goes to the Administrator lane, where the administrator reviews it with the recording and the transcript. A decision gateway then either blocks the number for 7 days, 30 days or for good, or dismisses the report. A blocked number is the one that the first gateway of the next call will reject.
