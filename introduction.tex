\chapter*{General Introduction}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{General Introduction}

\paragraph{General Context.}
Healthcare is going digital worldwide, and Tunisia is part of that shift. Patients expect medical services that are faster and easier to reach, and digital platforms have appeared to simplify contact between patients and healthcare professionals. Artificial intelligence and real-time communication tools in particular can modernize how medical appointments are managed and make care easier to access.

\paragraph{Problem Statement.}
Internet and mobile use keep growing, yet many patients in Tunisia still struggle to book a medical appointment. Booking usually means calling during working hours, going to the clinic in person, or using a web platform that not every user can handle. Language barriers, limited digital literacy, and urgent medical situations make the process slower and more stressful. Most existing solutions ask the patient to create an account or use a web portal. That leaves part of the population out, and these solutions do not offer immediate, simple help in several languages.

\paragraph{Proposed Solution.}
Pengguin, a company led by John Jang, built \textbf{Riaya} to address this problem. Riaya is an AI-powered doctor appointment platform based in Tunisia, designed to be ``AI-first'' and ``voice-first'': patients need no web portal or mobile application. They make a phone call, talk to a voice assistant, and receive follow-up messages on WhatsApp. The assistant speaks English, French, and Tunisian Arabic. It helps patients describe their need, choose a speciality, give their location, and book an appointment with the most suitable doctor. Doctors get a web dashboard to manage their practice: appointments, patients, invoices, medical documents, consultation recordings with transcripts, an AI assistant, and WhatsApp messages. Administrators verify doctors, monitor live AI calls, handle abusive callers, and supervise the platform.

\paragraph{Objectives of the Report.}
This report presents the Riaya project from a functional and a technical point of view. It aims to:
\begin{itemize}
    \item present the context of the project and the needs of each actor;
    \item describe the functional and non-functional requirements of the platform;
    \item analyze existing solutions and position Riaya against them;
    \item explain the project management methodology (Kanban) and the modeling diagrams used;
    \item detail the technical architecture, the technology stack, the implementation choices, and the deployment strategy;
    \item show and describe the main features of the platform with screenshots.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Structure of the Report.}
The report has three chapters. Chapter 1 introduces Pengguin and the Riaya product, sets out the functional and non-functional requirements, compares Riaya with existing solutions, and models the complete process in BPMN. Chapter 2 covers the project management methodology: what Kanban is, its advantages and disadvantages, and how it compares with other methodologies. It also presents the main modeling diagrams for the project. Chapter 3 describes the technical architecture and implementation of Riaya: the technology stack, annotated screenshots, security, and the deployment pipeline. A general conclusion summarizes the work, the challenges encountered, and the future perspectives.
